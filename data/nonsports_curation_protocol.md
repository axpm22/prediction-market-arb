# Ground-truth curation protocol (plan Phase 1.3 — the human task)

You are building the §18.5 ground-truth set: the file that decides whether the
AI matcher is ever allowed to run. Your judgments are the standard the machine
is measured against, so a wrong "same" here is worse than a wrong "different"
— when unsure, mark `different` or `unsure`. The instrument's guardrails
absorb missed matches; they cannot absorb false ones.

## Input / output

- Input: `data/nonsports_candidates.yaml` (generated by the Phase 1.2 matcher;
  each entry = one Kalshi market + one Polymarket market the blocker paired,
  with both venues' full titles, resolution dates, strikes, and links).
- Output: your verdicts, appended to the same entries as `verdict:` +
  `verdict_note:`. Confirmed `same` pairs are promoted to
  `data/nonsports_map.yaml` (the rule-11 trusted path); everything you judge —
  including `different` — stays in the candidates file as held-out truth.

## The one question

**"Would every possible state of the world settle these two markets the same
way?"** Not "are they about the same topic" — about the same topic is how V1
made fake tennis edges. Settlement-identical or nothing.

## The checklist per pair (aim: <2 minutes each)

1. **Resolution source.** Kalshi cites its source in the market rules; PM
   cites its in the resolution criteria. BLS vs BLS = fine. "AP calls the
   race" vs "official certification" = **different** (2020 proved it).
2. **Threshold and direction.** `>= 3.0%` vs `> 3.0%` = different. "above
   80°F" vs "80°F or higher" = different at exactly 80. Check the boundary.
3. **Units and rounding.** CPI MoM rounded to 0.1% vs unrounded; °F vs °C;
   seasonally adjusted vs not (U-3 vs U-6 unemployment!). Any mismatch =
   different.
4. **Date and cutoff.** Same measurement period AND same announcement/expiry
   convention. "In July" vs "by July 31, 11:59 PM ET" — check which print
   settles it if the release is revised or delayed.
5. **Entity precision.** "Trump wins the nomination" vs "Trump wins the
   presidency"; "a Republican" vs "Trump". Politics one-offs die here — read
   both rule texts fully, not the titles.
6. **Framing inversion.** A Kalshi NO can be a PM YES on the complement. If
   settlement-identical under inversion, mark `same` with
   `inverted: true` in the note — the matcher needs to know the polarity.
7. **Edge cases clause.** What happens on cancellation, postponement, data
   revision, or "other" outcomes? If the two rulebooks diverge on any
   contingency you can imagine actually happening, mark `different` and name
   the contingency in the note — these notes seed the settlement matrix.

## Verdicts

- `same` — settlement-identical in all worlds. Promoted to the map.
- `different` — any checklist item fails. Say which item in the note.
- `unsure` — you couldn't determine it from the rule texts in ~3 minutes.
  Never guess; unsure pairs are excluded from both map and truth set.

## Targets and hygiene

- **≥50 `same` and ≥30 `different`** verdicts (the negatives are what measure
  the AI's false-positive rate — they are not wasted work).
- Spread across all three categories; weather/econ will go fast (strikes match
  or they don't), politics is where the reading time goes.
- Work in 15–20 minute sessions; judgment quality decays fast.
- Never edit a past verdict in place — append a corrected entry with a note
  (append-only applies to truth too).
- Log actual time spent; it goes in the writeup ("N pairs, M hours" is a
  methodology fact reviewers respect).
